Track 9 (Local Business & Industry)

One view of stock, batches and sales channels

No coding needed

The problem

A small snack brand sells online, on Amazon, to wholesale buyers, through a few local shops and at a Saturday market. Its stock sits in its own warehouse and at a shipping company's warehouse. Every movement is typed by hand into one spreadsheet. The owner can't easily see which product sells where, or which batches are getting close to their best-by date.

Who it's for

The owner and one staff member. They aren't tech specialists, and they have no budget for a monthly inventory app.

Build at minimum

A simple dashboard or tool that:

  • shows stock by product, batch and location;
  • shows units sold by channel, by week or month;
  • lists the batches to sell or move first, and where they should go under the shelf-life rules below.

Stretch goals

  • Compare the hand-kept ledger with the online store's order export and the warehouse's stock count, and explain any differences.
  • Import those files directly instead of retyping.
  • Answer plain questions, like "what should go to the market this Saturday?"

The data

Everything here is made up for this event. It is shaped like a real small brand's records, with no customer names, emails or addresses.

Good to know

Shelf-life rules (made up for this event)

How many days must be left before the best-by date when stock goes to each channel. These rules are invented. They are not any real company's policy.

Minimum days left by channel
ChannelMinimum days left
Amazon warehouse120 when shipped; pull or discount below 60
Wholesale buyers90
Online store (ships from the shipping warehouse)45
Local shops75 at drop-off; collect below 30
Saturday marketAny. Sell the shortest-dated stock here first
How the ledger works
  • One row per batch moved. Types: opening balance, receipt, transfer, sale, return, adjustment.
  • The channel is filled in for sales only.
  • On market days, stock goes out in the morning and what's left comes back the same day.
  • Local shops report sales once a month, so their sales show up at month end.
  • The ledger is kept by hand, like most small-business sheets.
