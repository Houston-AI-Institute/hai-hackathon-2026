Track 5 (Food Access & Resource Navigation) · Houston Food Bank

Where should the food bank listen first?

Partner challenge No coding needed

Read this first. This data describes areas, never people. It is nonpartisan: turnout counts only, with no party, candidate or vote choice. It is not for food distribution, eligibility, pantry siting, decisions about any person, or choosing where to run voter registration or get-out-the-vote drives. A low score is not low interest. Missing data is not missing civic life.

The problem

The Houston Food Bank wants to hear from the communities it serves, through focus groups and outreach conversations across its 10 counties: Austin, Brazoria, Chambers, Fort Bend, Harris, Liberty, San Jacinto, Trinity, Walker and Waller. It can't be everywhere at once. Build a tool that helps it decide where to listen first, and explain each choice in plain language with the numbers behind it.

Who it's for

Food bank staff who plan focus groups and outreach conversations across the 10 counties.

What to build

The minimum is a target, not a rule. Judges score every team with the same five criteria. All of it works in a spreadsheet, so no coding is needed.

Build at minimum

  • Your own score. Pick up to 3 columns from the table, give each a weight, and say why. We suggest voter turnout (P1) counts for at least half, and civic organizations for a quarter or less.
  • Grey out the rows marked "not ranked", and mark the rows with a caution flag.
  • A shortlist of 8 places across at least 4 counties. Include both ends: places to "listen first" (lower measured participation) and places to "work with" (higher measured participation). Include both city and rural places.
  • A 2 to 3 line reason for each place, citing its numbers.
  • The AI part: an AI assistant drafts each reason from that row's numbers. Your team checks every number against the table, and one slide shows a correction you made.
  • A one-page "how to read this" for food bank staff.

Stretch goals

  • Does your shortlist hold up with a second, different weighting? Show what changes.
  • Group ZIP areas into community types with neutral labels ("Type A", "Type B") and show the numbers behind each type.
  • An English and Spanish focus-group guide, adapted from a fixed, neutral template. AI may change only the logistics, the reading level and the language. A person on your team reviews every guide. TODO: template link to come.

Team roles that work

Data, score sheet, map, responsible use and focus-group guide, and pitch.

Start here

  • No code? Open zcta_civic.xlsx in Google Sheets or Excel. Read the read_me sheet first. The not-ranked rows are already grey and sorted to the bottom.
  • Python? Row 1 of each CSV is a use statement, so skip it, and keep ZIP codes as text: pd.read_csv(path, skiprows=1, dtype={"ZCTA": str, "county_main": str}). See open a Colab notebook and load a CSV.
  • Start with the county table to see the whole region, then look at ZIP areas inside one county.

Good to know

Rules for this challenge
  • Areas, never people. Don't look up, match or name any person, voter or household.
  • Nonpartisan. Use only the turnout counts in this table. Don't add party, candidate, primary or vote-choice data, and don't download candidate files yourself.
  • Don't scrape neighborhood association lists. The City of Houston's list includes names and emails, so it was left out on purpose.
  • Your shortlist is for listening: focus groups and outreach conversations. It doesn't choose where to run voter registration or get-out-the-vote. That is the food bank's decision with its counsel.
  • No prompt or AI output may guess race, ethnicity, citizenship or attitudes from area numbers.
  • Describe areas, not people. Don't call any area "disengaged", a "civic desert" or "underperforming".
  • On your slides, use county views only. Your ZIP-level shortlist goes in your team folder for the food bank, not on your slides. Presentations are recorded.
What P1 means
  • P1 is the average of 2022 and 2024 general-election turnout, divided by adult citizens (minus adults held in correctional facilities). It's a reference measure, not a score of a community.
  • P1_rank runs from 0 to 100 among ranked ZIP areas. 100 is the highest measured participation.
  • The adult citizen count is a survey estimate, so each rank has a range (rank_p05 to rank_p95), about 15 points wide in a typical area. If two areas' ranges overlap, don't treat them as different.
  • Voting districts and ZIP areas don't line up, so each district's counts are shared out to ZIP areas by where adults live. County totals are exact. The ZIP split is an estimate, weakest where the SPLIT caution flag is set and in rural areas.
  • P1 can be above 1 in fast-growing areas, where 2024 voters outnumber the older survey estimate of adults. These rows have the GROWTH flag.
Why 65 ZIP areas aren't ranked
  • An area isn't ranked when the measure would mislead: less than half its land is in the 10 counties, much of its population lives outside them, it has fewer than 1,500 residents, it has no usable adult citizen estimate, or many residents live in group quarters or correctional facilities. The reason is in not_ranked_reason.
  • Not ranked is not low. These are areas the data can't describe well, not areas with low participation. Together they hold about 4 percent of the region's registered voters.
  • In Walker, Liberty and San Jacinto counties most ZIP areas are too small or cross county lines, so a ZIP ranking doesn't describe most of the county. Work at county level there.
  • Rows with a caution flag are ranked but need care. The read_me sheet explains each flag.
Civic organizations and neighborhood conditions
  • civic_orgs_n counts IRS-registered community, neighborhood, service club and citizen participation groups. Homeowner and property owner associations are counted separately in hoa_like_n.
  • Groups are counted at their IRS mailing address, which may not be where they meet. Groups with PO box ZIP codes are lost. Churches, mutual aid groups, tenant groups and informal groups often don't file with the IRS, so they aren't counted.
  • There is no public neighborhood association list for the region outside the City of Houston.
  • 77 ZIP areas show 0 civic organizations. That means none were found in this file, not that none exist.
  • The five neighborhood conditions (same house as a year ago, owner-occupied homes, high school diploma, households that aren't limited-English, broadband) are context only. They aren't in P1.
