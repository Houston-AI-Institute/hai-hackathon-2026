Track 9 (Local Business & Industry)

Which care facilities to approach first

Some coding helps

The problem

A small, non-medical home-safety company gets a patient's home ready before they leave a hospital, rehab hospital or nursing facility: grab bars, ramps, lighting and a safety check. It then supports the first weeks at home. The company wants 2 pilot programs with facilities, but has little time to sell. Which Houston-area facilities should it approach first, and why?

Who it's for

The company's owner, who decides where to spend selling time. And a facility's discharge planner (the staff member who arranges care when a patient goes home), who would read the pitch.

What to build

The minimum is a target, not a rule. Judges score every team with the same five criteria.

Build at minimum

  • A ranked list (say, the top 15) from the data files. Rank one facility type at a time.
  • A score with weights you state. Signals could include: patients who live in the company's area, discharge-to-community rates (patients who go home, not to another facility), readmissions (patients back in hospital soon after leaving), volume, and chain size.
  • A plain-language "why" for each signal, with the file, column and time period it came from.
  • A short pitch draft for each of your top 3, using only numbers from the files.

Stretch goals

  • A map of where families might need help at home, by ZIP or census tract (a small area of a few thousand people). For example: people 65+ living alone, older homes, mobility needs.
  • A list of home health agencies that could refer patients.
  • A question-and-answer helper that says when the files can't answer.
  • A fairness check: does your ranking skip areas with less access to care?

The data

All public, facility-level or area-level data. Filter it to Harris, Fort Bend, Brazoria, Galveston and Montgomery counties. No patient-level data and no names of people.

Good to know

Rules for this challenge
  • Don't contact facilities, agencies or the company. No calls, emails, visits or messages, during or after the event.
  • Don't add data about people: no staff names, online profile lookups or patient stories.
  • Pitch text uses only numbers from these files, with the source and time period. Don't invent results or reviews.
  • If your tool is asked something the files can't answer, it should say so.
  • Your code is shared as open source. Keep your ranked list and pitch in your team folder, not in a public repo or post.
Reading the data
  • The files cover Original Medicare only. People in Medicare Advantage are not in most of them.
  • In the service-area file, * means 1 to 10 cases. Treat it as "small", not zero.
  • Time periods differ between files. Show the period next to every number you use.
  • Small facilities have wide uncertainty ranges. Two facilities whose ranges overlap may not really differ.
  • A measure is a reason to start a conversation, not proof that a facility needs or will buy anything.
What strong tools usually do
  • Show the measure, the period, and any ties when they rank facilities.
  • Say so when the files can't answer a question, rather than guess.
  • Still answer questions the files can answer, even when they sound sensitive.
  • Use numbers in the pitch that anyone can find in the named file.
