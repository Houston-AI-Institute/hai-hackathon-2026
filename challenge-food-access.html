Track 5 (Food Access & Resource Navigation)

Find food help near you

Some coding helps

The problem

Families who need food help struggle to work out where to go, whether they qualify, and whether they can get there. Opening hours, transport, documents and language all get in the way. The information exists, but it's spread across many agencies.

Who it's for

Families in Harris, Fort Bend and Brazoria counties looking for food help, and the volunteers and caseworkers who help them.

What to build

Build at minimum

A tool that:

  • takes an address or ZIP code and shows nearby food resources (school and summer meal sites, SNAP stores, WIC offices, and the sample pantry list);
  • explains who qualifies, in plain language;
  • says how to get there by bus where it can;
  • says "I'm not sure" and points to 211 or the provider when it can't answer.

Stretch goals

  • Spanish, plus one more language.
  • Map areas that are far from food resources and poorly served by buses.
  • Filter by opening hours.

The data

Public data only, with no personal information. File names and row counts come with the data pack.

Good to know

Ideas to try

Test your tool with lookups like "a family of 4 with no car, Saturday morning" plus an address or ZIP. There's no single right answer. Show what your tool does, and where it isn't sure.

Using the data
  • Say where every piece of data came from.
  • Credit METRO if you use the bus data.
  • Don't put anyone's personal information into your tool or into an AI tool.
  • Opening hours and rules change. Tell users to check with the provider.
