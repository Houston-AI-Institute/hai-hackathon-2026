Track 9 (Local Business & Industry) · Opt in

Learn from offshore incident reports

Some coding helps Opt in: real injury reports

Before you open the data: These are real reports about people who were hurt while working offshore. If you're under 18, you need a parent's or guardian's OK first. Even with an OK, you can choose to work on another part of your team's project instead.

The problem

Offshore teams file hundreds of incident reports, but the lessons stay buried in free text. A safety team wants to learn from past incidents without reading every report by hand.

Who it's for

Safety teams in offshore oil and gas and other industries, who investigate incidents and try to stop them happening again.

What to build

The minimum is a target, not a rule. Judges score every team with the same five criteria.

Build at minimum

A tool that helps a safety team learn from past incidents. Pick one:

  • spot patterns in what caused incidents;
  • find similar past incidents for a new report;
  • flag reports that are missing key details.

Then show honestly how well it works.

Stretch goals

  • Do more than one of the three.
  • Label some rows in the gold folder by hand, compare your results with them, and say how many rows you checked.
  • Show the user why your tool gave each answer.

The data

Built from public US federal offshore incident reports. Some cells are real report text and some are made up; every cell says which. The repo's README explains how. Names were removed, but some real names may remain. Don't copy any names into your project.

Good to know

Tips for honest results
  • The way investigators named causes changed over the years. Compare within one time period.
  • Report how many rows you tested on, not just a score.
  • Labels made by an AI model are not the truth. Check them against rows your team labeled by hand.
If the reports upset you

You can stop reading at any time and talk to your mentor or a volunteer.
