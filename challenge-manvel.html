Track 3 (Permitting & Ordinance Assistant)

Help Manvel residents find answers in the city's published information

Some coding helps

The problem

Manvel residents ask city staff the same questions again and again. Can I build on my lot? Can I split my land after I buy it? Is this a city rule or a county rule? The answers are already published, but they're spread across the city's website, the Code of Ordinances, permit forms, maps and Brazoria County's rules.

Who it's for

Manvel residents, including older residents, and small builders. Also the city's permitting staff, who answer these questions every day.

Build at minimum

A tool that:

  • answers common property and permit questions in plain language, using only the city's and county's published information;
  • shows the source for every answer, with a link;
  • says "I'm not sure" when it can't answer, and sends people to the city's Development Services department (opens in a new tab).

Stretch goals

  • Address lookup: is this address inside city limits, in the ETJ (the area just outside city limits), or only in the county?
  • Point people to the city's Development Guide for their kind of project.
  • Work by voice, like a phone call.

The data

All of it is public, on the city's and county's own websites. Nothing to download from us.

Practice questions

Written by the event team from the city's published pages. Not checked by city staff. Ask your tool these, then check its answers against the sources above.

  1. I want to put a shed in my backyard. Do I need a permit?
  2. Do I need a permit to put up a fence?
  3. How do I check on a permit I already applied for?
  4. I bought 5 acres. Can I split off 1 acre so my daughter can build a house?
  5. Is my house inside Manvel city limits, or just in Brazoria County?
  6. What is my property zoned, and can I run a small business from home?

On the day, judges ask every team's tool the same set of common resident questions. You won't see them in advance. A good answer is right, shows where it came from, and sends people to city staff when it should.

Some questions can't be answered from the published pages. Your tool should say so, not guess.

Good to know

Using the data
  • Use only what the city and county publish. Don't make up rules.
  • Show the date of each source. Rules change, and the online code can be behind.
  • Be gentle with the city's website. Download each page or file once. Don't run tools that hit the site again and again.
  • Parcel data can show owners' names. Don't put names or anyone's personal information into your tool, your slides or an AI tool.
What happens after the hackathon
  • Teams build one-day prototypes. The city doesn't host, support or guarantee anything built on the day.
  • Nothing is released under the city's name without the city's review.
  • Any decision about using an idea stays with the city.

How it's judged

Same five criteria as every track. For this challenge, judges also look at how right your tool's answers are, whether it shows the right sources, whether it sends people to staff when it should, and how easy it is for older residents to use. How judging works
